## 1. What the research says (and what your fluff is missing)

**What recruits actually scan for** (EVE University recruiting guide, "How to Recruit" forum guide): a clear focus — not "we do everything"; where you live (high/low/null/WH); timezone; what's *different* about you; honesty about what you've done vs. where you're going; one obvious next step (Discord link). Posts that list generic features (SRP, ore buyback, "friendly community") all look the same; the ones that get replies say who they are.

**Why people join a group** (social-identity research on guilds): they are looking for an identity to wear, people like them, and a role that matters. Belonging comes from *shared purpose + visible contribution + recognition*. Three levers that your draft doesn't use yet:

1. **Founder effect.** Early members of a growing community get something no established alliance can offer: their fingerprints on it. "Founding member" status that stays visible forever is one of the most effective engagement tools in guild management. Say it explicitly and make it real (a permanent `Founder` role/title).
2. **"You", not "we".** Your three texts are almost entirely about the Orlovs. A reader wants to picture *themselves* on day one: what they'd be doing, who they'd be talking to, what their work turns into. Every paragraph should answer "what's in it for the reader" — including non-ISK things like influence and being heard.
3. **Honesty as a hook, not a weakness.** "We are three weeks old and one corp" filters out people who want a finished product and attracts the ones who want to build. Pair it with a concrete, dated plan so "new" reads as "momentum", not "empty".

**Tone.** The capitalist-dynasty fluff is a strong identity — keep it as flavour (one or two lines), not as the body. A reader needs to hear a human voice underneath it; the forum is full of lore-only posts nobody replies to.

**Format conventions on the EVE forums Recruitment Center:** title carries the essentials in tags (`[EUTZ]`, `[Highsec/Lowsec/Null]`, `[Indy]`, `[New alliance]`); short paragraphs; a bullet block for hard facts; Discord link at the end; bump the thread regularly (reply "bump" or an update every few days — updates about progress are better than bare bumps, and they reinforce the "growing" message).

**What to avoid:** walls of lore; promising capitals/SRP/sov you don't have; "all timezones"; vague "contact leadership" with no name; more than ~400 words before the bullets.
